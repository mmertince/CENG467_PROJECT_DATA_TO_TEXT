1. Technical Differences

The primary difference between BLEU and BLEURT lies in how they 
measure the similarity between a candidate (model output) and 
a reference (target).

                Feature,BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy),                         BLEURT (BERT-based Evaluation)
Methodology,    Lexical Overlap: Counts matching n-grams (sequences of 1-4 words).,     Semantic Embedding: Uses a pre-trained Transformer (BERT) to compare meaning.
Logic,          Precision-oriented; requires exact word-for-word matches.,               Neural-based; recognizes synonyms and paraphrases.
Flexibility,    Rigid; penalizes correct synonyms or varied word orders.,                  Flexible; rewards sentences that carry the same meaning.
Judgment,       Often correlates poorly with human perception of quality.,              Specifically trained to correlate with human quality ratings.


2. Which Score is Better for This Task?

For the Table-to-Text task (ToTTo), BLEURT is the superior metric. 
In data-to-text generation, the goal is to convert structured data into a natural-sounding sentence.
There are dozens of linguistically "correct" ways to describe a single row of a table. For example:

Target: "Daniel Henry Chamberlain was the 76th Governor of South Carolina from 1874."
Model: "From 1874, Daniel Henry Chamberlain served as the governor for South Carolina."

BLEU would assign a lower score here because the word order changed and "served as the" was added.
BLEURT, however, recognizes that the factual integrity and meaning are identical,
providing a much fairer assessment of the model's actual performance.


3. Failure to Reflect QWEN Model Success

Yes, BLEU is highly likely to fail to reflect the successful outputs of the QWEN model. 
Large Language Models (LLMs) like Qwen-4B are trained on diverse web data, 
making them highly fluent and prone to creative paraphrasing. 
When Qwen generates a technically perfect and highly readable sentence that happens 
to use different phrasing than the human-written target, 
BLEU will "fail" by assigning it a low score.

In our results, our BLEURT score (0.4922) is significantly higher than many supervised baselines 
on the leaderboard (some around 0.35), even though our BLEU is lower. 
This indicates that while Qwen doesn't "mimic" the reference's exact vocabulary, 
it is highly successful at capturing the table's meaning, 
a success that BLEU is mathematically unable to see.
